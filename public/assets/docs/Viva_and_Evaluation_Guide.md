# JU Major Project — viva and evaluation preparation
**Review edition • 27 November 2026.** Reproduce the calculations and CAD yourself before submitting. Use the slides as a defense aid, not as evidence of completed tests.

## 60-second opening
My project investigates controlled drying of paddy in a static hollow-cylinder chamber. The outer wall is a solar absorber, the grain occupies an annulus, and a perforated inner shell permits possible exchange with a central air core. The modified arrangement forces clean air from a bottom plenum rather than relying on natural convection. The nominal 100 kg case removes 9.30 kg of water from 22 to 14% wet basis. The 670/250 mm reference shell geometry fits the assumed volume. I have found an energy inconsistency in the alternate 80°C calculation, so I do not claim a six-hour drying time. My proposed first trial is at 40–45°C air, with distributed measurements and an approved grain-temperature limit. In 4.2 I intend to characterize flow, calibrate the drying model and assess uniformity and energy use.

## Questions and defensible answers
1. **Why drying before milling?** Paddy is harvested wet; appropriate moisture management reduces deterioration and protects milling quality. Hulling is mechanical and outside this research scope.
2. **Why not 8 kg water removed?** 14% is of the final wet mass, not initial mass. Conserved dry solids =78 kg; final mass=78/.86=90.70; water removed=9.30 kg.
3. **Why hollow cylinder?** Compact annulus, potential solar wall gain and controllable central airflow path. It is not inherently superior; compare moisture uniformity and pressure with a baseline.
4. **What is actually original?** The particular low-cost design and proposed comparison of open/capped core and solar-on/off. Existing radial, crossflow and rotating solar dryers mean no universal novelty claim.
5. **How did you size 670 mm?** Required V=100/560=.17857 m³; divide by .6 m, solve annulus equation with r=.125 m; add 2 mm wall per side. Exact OD=668.41 mm, rounded=670; actual nominal capacity=100.56 kg.
6. **Why 24 kg and 100 kg?** Distinct stages. 24 kg is a smaller first-build option, 100 kg is analytical scale-up, not the same prototype.
7. **Why forced rather than natural convection?** Natural buoyancy is weather-dependent and may not overcome packed-bed resistance; measured fan/system curve allows control. Flow distribution still must be tested.
8. **What is relative humidity vs humidity ratio?** RH is partial pressure divided by saturation pressure; humidity ratio is mass vapour per mass dry air. Sensible heating at fixed water content lowers RH, not humidity ratio.
9. **How was airflow calculated?** Water rate=9.30/21600 kg/s; assumed Δω=.02538; dry-air flow=.01697 kg/s; moist-air specific volume ~1.031 m³/kg at 80°C; volume ~63 m³/h. This is conditional, not a proven required fan duty.
10. **Why 61 vs 63?** 61 is the source rounded dry ideal-gas approximation; 63 uses consistent moist-air volume at the assumed inlet. Neither is measured.
11. **Why reject 80°C as first-trial setpoint?** The conservative 40–45°C strategy is in the later report; air temperature is not grain temperature, but hot air can damage grain. No hotter mode approved. Late-stage quality may require lower grain limit near 40°C.
12. **What is the energy contradiction?** h_in≈130.17, h_out≈159.35 kJ/kg dry air. Positive rise requires net heat plus changes in stored grain/water energy; small gross solar input cannot by itself establish the assumed outlet. Complete balance, not a guessed heater rating.
13. **What is useful heat?** Approximate latent 22.38 MJ plus grain sensible 2.55 MJ =24.93 MJ. 50% additive allowance →37.40 MJ over 6h ≈1.73 kW; not measured efficiency.
14. **Why not add 0.88 kW to 1.73 kW?** Possible overlap in energy boundary. Moist-air enthalpy already includes latent terms; define control volumes and storage before adding loads.
15. **What does a black shell contribute?** Illustrative gross absorbed 0.90×600×.67×.6=217 W; actual net gain smaller and variable. It is supplemental, not primary guaranteed heat.
16. **Why thin metal conduction is not bottleneck?** Resistance ln(ro/ri)/(2πkL) ~3.5e−5 K/W. A hypothetical 5K metal drop implies a huge flux that actual solar input cannot supply. External and bed-side transfer dominate.
17. **Why Ergun?** Screening correlation for viscous/inertial resistance through porous beds. At legacy u=.057 m/s about 65 Pa. Elongated kernels, fines, plenum, floor, perforations and duct add uncertainty; measure loaded and empty curves.
18. **Why is blower wattage not sufficient?** Fan must deliver required flow at actual static pressure. Bed-only shaft power ~2.6 W; duct, floor/exchanger and motor losses not included. Use real fan curve.
19. **Why 1.5 mm holes and 6 mm pitch?** Proposed retention and open fraction 5.67%. 4 mm starter holes are not justified. Test smallest kernels, broken grain and dust; seam, floor and support design remain open.
20. **How would you model in SolidWorks?** Top Plane, MMGS, concentric circles with diameter dimensions, extrude 600 mm separate outer and inner parts, assign provisional materials, assemble concentric and coincident bases; section and measure. STEP generated with CadQuery, not native SolidWorks.
21. **Why SS304 inner?** Moist air and grain contact favor corrosion resistance and cleanability. Verify actual grade, finish and contact compliance. Outer coated surface is exterior only.
22. **How does drying rate change?** Grain often dries in falling-rate regime as internal moisture transport controls; equilibrium depends on T/RH; MR Newton/Page are empirical fits. No k, n or D_eff measured.
23. **Can mass Biot prove the airflow is enough?** No. Bi compares internal/external resistances with consistent concentration basis, but bed distribution and moisture equilibrium are separate. No coefficient or flow was measured.
24. **What are the failure risks?** Air bypass through core, uneven moisture, overtemperature, clogging, electrical or fire hazards, flue leakage. Cold-flow tests, airflow interlock, independent cutoff, temperature map and supervised operation.
25. **What will you measure?** Calibrated air flow, pressure drop, plenum/outlet T and RH, grain T at 3 heights ×2 radii, mass and MC vs time, irradiance and energy. Log all conditions and repeated runs.
26. **What validates your model?** Compare independent gravimetric loss with integrated dry-air humidity pickup, measured pressure curve and predicted values; fit kinetics and test on held-out run. Report uncertainty and spatial moisture spread.
27. **How measure efficiency?** SEC = measured supplied energy / kg water removed; define electricity, fuel and solar boundaries. If using useful-energy efficiency, state exactly what is counted and report uncertainty.
28. **What is completed?** Concept, historic CAD/instructions, illustrations/video, October preliminary calculation and theory reading documented. Current package adds AI-assisted arithmetic checks, compatible shell CAD, illustrations and interface. No fabricated or tested prototype or CFD/FEA.
29. **What are next semester deliverables?** Approved detailed design, cold-flow/thermal experiments if feasible, validated model, comparison of alternatives, optimization and complete report/presentation.
30. **What must the supervisor decide?** Scale, title, control and grain limits, flow path and core cap, material and retention, heating boundary, available facilities and milestones. Do not say the supervisor approved these unless confirmed.
